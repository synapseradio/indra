## Why

The `syndicated-actors-conceptual-alignment` initiative re-founds INDRA on the Syndicated Actor Model: actors that publish typed assertions, observe patterns over a dataspace, and take turns under a deterministic dispatch rule, with isolation fixed by attenuated capabilities. The four model specs still describe the older delegation-and-persona design — `await:` / `store_in:` / `&result` call stacks, `&context` dotted-path state, `say:`-routing by name, and a stunted persona tied to a single inference call. One spec also still carries the language-era name `interpreter-runtime`, from when INDRA was modeled as a language with an interpreter rather than a TypeScript framework with a runtime. They contradict the direction the initiative records and the vocabulary the `README.md` and `CLAUDE.md` adopt. This change brings the normative specs into line with their own canonical coordination document before further work builds on the stale surface.

## What Changes

- Re-found three of the four model specs (`framework-core-runtime`, `context-state`, `signal-system`) on SAM: assertions, observe-by-pattern, capabilities with attenuation, transactional turns, supervision via retraction, and handle-plus-`caused-by` provenance. Each keeps the requirements the initiative names as salvage and drops the delegation-and-persona-spawn requirements it names for removal. The fourth model spec, `inference-layer`, is retired rather than re-founded: INDRA does not perform inference, so its salvageable contracts move to a new `inference-boundary` edge capability and the rest are dropped.
- **BREAKING**: Rename the `interpreter-runtime` capability to `framework-core-runtime`. The "interpreter" name encodes the rejected language framing; the framework's deterministic execution core is a runtime, not an interpreter. The spec directory, the capability map in `openspec/specs/README.md`, and every cross-reference move to the new name.
- **BREAKING**: Remove the delegation call stack (`await:` / `store_in:` / `&result`), `say:`-routing by name, and the `become:` persona-spawn from `framework-core-runtime`. Remove the `&context` / `&user` / `&signals` namespaces and dotted-path addressing from `context-state`. Remove the `as:`-on-one-call persona form from `inference-layer`. These are observable surface removals, not internal refactors.
- Redefine **persona** as agent configuration, and give it a new `agent-configuration` capability. A persona is an agent's authored configuration — its instructions, its available tools, and its other settings. A persona composes by deterministic data merge, without orchestration; an agent, an inference call in a loop, is parameterized by a persona. This keeps persona out of the runtime-spawn role (the removed `become:` and `as:` forms) while making it a first-class, composable construct. The emergent character a persona produces, and its voice, stay mental-model terms and appear in no spec.
- Retire `inference-layer` and stand up **`inference-boundary`** as the validated edge to the external model, per fork F5. INDRA does not perform inference; the boundary is the one marked place a turn hands a request to an external model and validates the typed value that crosses back. The typed-value-validated and declared-output-shape contracts move here; the mandated leading reasoning field and the `<...>` host-function form are dropped.
- Settle the deterministic dispatch rule and its three duties — inter-turn order, intra-turn batch consistency, and cycle termination — as a requirement of the re-founded `framework-core-runtime` spec, harvested from `define-runtime-supervision-model`. This also decides whether cyclic observation is permitted and what bounds its convergence.
- Fold supervision into `framework-core-runtime` as well: a retracted assertion signals failure and a supervisor reacts, reframing `define-runtime-supervision-model`'s typed-failure-to-supervisor as failure-as-retraction. The dispatch rule and supervision share the runtime's serial dispatch, so they belong in the same capability.
- Introduce a `program-validation` capability for the load-time gate, harvested from `validate-program-at-load`. The gate stays, and its checks change: totality becomes reaction coverage, reference resolution becomes capability resolution, and initial-state validation becomes initial-assertion validation.
- Resolve the five design forks (F1 eventual-consistency substrate under deterministic dispatch, F2 actor-as-unit with entity collapsed, F3 network layer left behind, F4 no silent discard, F5 inference call as turn body) in the spec text so the specs state the resolved positions rather than the open question.
- Archive the four model changes (`framework-native-contracts`, `scope-context-by-actor-subtree`, `define-runtime-supervision-model`, `validate-program-at-load`), harvesting their reusable requirements into the re-founded specs first.
- Scrub language-era residue from the specs: dead colon-form references in the mechanics artifacts (`package-boundaries`, `package-scaffold`, `git-hooks`, `module-resolution`, `extract-inference-adapter`, `clarify-module-resolution`), and phrasing in the model specs that frames INDRA as a language, such as `inference-layer`'s "protocol language".

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `program-validation`: the load-time gate, harvested from the `validate-program-at-load` change as its owning change is archived. It rejects an invalid program before the first turn. Its checks are reaction coverage (replacing totality), capability resolution (replacing reference resolution), and initial-assertion validation (replacing initial-state validation).
- `agent-configuration`: the layer that defines a **persona** as an agent's authored configuration — instructions, available tools, and other settings — and the rules by which personas compose as data, without orchestration. An agent is parameterized by a persona; composing personas is data composition, distinct from the side-effect scheduling that composing agents requires.
- `inference-boundary`: the validated edge to the external model, the runtime counterpart to `program-validation`'s load-time gate. A turn hands a request to an external model, and the boundary validates the returned value against a declared output shape, retries a parse failure, and fails the turn before commit when retries are exhausted. It holds the typed-value-validated and declared-output-shape contracts harvested from the retired `inference-layer`.

### Modified Capabilities

- `framework-core-runtime` (renamed from `interpreter-runtime`): the turn cycle and transactional-boundary commit, the total deterministic evaluator, halt-not-gated-by-trace, the one-generic-interpreter-reads-data idea, and loop-frame reification are kept and reframed as SAM turns over a dataspace. The delegation call stack, `say:`-routing, and the `become:` persona-spawn are removed; persona itself is redefined as agent configuration, not a runtime spawn. The deterministic dispatch rule (three duties) and supervision-via-retraction are added here, harvested from `define-runtime-supervision-model`. The rename is part of this capability's delta.
- `context-state`: the transactional whiteboard, staged-versus-immediate writes, and serialized contending commits are kept and reframed as local deterministic replication. The `&`-namespaces and dotted-path addressing are removed in favor of typed assertions and dataflow fields.
- `signal-system`: the mailbox, turn-boundary observation, and the runtime-owned signals namespace are kept and reframed as runtime-asserted facts. The `*command`, three-mode classification, and interjection model are reframed around the human as an asserting-and-observing actor.

### Removed Capabilities

- `inference-layer`: retired. It framed INDRA as the generative surface that performs inference, which INDRA does not do. Its salvageable contracts move on: typed-value validation and declared output shapes to `inference-boundary`, and persona to `agent-configuration`. The mandated leading reasoning field is dropped because INDRA is not a reasoning framework, and the `<...>` pure-function and host-function form is dropped because pure computation is ordinary TypeScript. The live spec directory and its `openspec/specs/README.md` map entry are removed when this change applies.

## Impact

- **Specs**: delta specs for `framework-core-runtime` (renamed from `interpreter-runtime`), `context-state`, and `signal-system`; three new specs, `program-validation`, `agent-configuration`, and `inference-boundary`; and the retirement of `inference-layer`. The `interpreter-runtime` to `framework-core-runtime` rename and the `inference-layer` removal both carry through the spec directory and the `openspec/specs/README.md` capability map. The supervision and dispatch-rule content from `define-runtime-supervision-model` lands in `framework-core-runtime` rather than a standalone spec, because its owning change is being archived.
- **Changes archived**: `framework-native-contracts`, `scope-context-by-actor-subtree`, `define-runtime-supervision-model`, `validate-program-at-load` move to `openspec/changes/archive/` after their requirements are harvested. The `define-runtime-supervision-model` dispatch-rule and supervision content lands in `framework-core-runtime`; the `validate-program-at-load` load-time gate lands in the new `program-validation` capability.
- **Mechanics artifacts**: editorial scrub of dead colon-form references in `package-boundaries`, `package-scaffold`, `git-hooks`, `module-resolution`, `extract-inference-adapter`, `clarify-module-resolution`. No requirement changes.
- **Vocabulary**: redefining persona as agent configuration reverses the current `README.md` and `CLAUDE.md` stance that persona is mental-model-only and belongs in no spec; the canonical vocabulary needs updating so persona names the authored configuration, with the emergent character and its voice kept as the mental-model result that appears in no spec. No code changes are in scope here — this is spec re-founding ahead of the runtime refactor the initiative anticipates.
- **Out of this change's reach**: distribution, persistence, and the entity-level granularity (forks F2 and F3) stay deferred; the specs state them as deferred, not designed. A first-class slot for per-run injected configuration (the equivalent of Mastra's RequestContext) is noted for a later change, alongside the initiative's open question on how a developer expresses how much is determined.
